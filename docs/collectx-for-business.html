The money model

CollectX already runs as a managed-escrow marketplace. A buyer pays the platform balance through Stripe Checkout; funds are held until delivery is confirmed (or 14 days pass); a Stripe Transfer then pays the seller’s connected account. The platform keeps the fee spread. Grading scans are a separate line (£2.99 / 10, 3 free).

Today the spread is 5% from the buyer, 0% from the seller — deliberately soft to seed peer-to-peer supply. Stores are a different animal: a business channel with daily volume. They earn a badge, qualified demand and tooling; in exchange they pay a seller commission collectors don’t. Every fee stays in one tunable place (marketplace_fee_config), now with a per-account override so a store’s rate can differ without a redeploy.

Five revenue lines, in the order they switch on

  1. Buyer protection fee (live). 5% on top of item price — applies to store sales too, so a store sale earns buyer 5% + seller commission.
  2. Store seller commission (Phase 1). Per-sale, configurable in bps, per-account override. Founder rate 3% to onboard the first cohort; 8% standard after. Charged out of the seller payout — no change to how buyers pay.
  3. Promoted listings & storefronts (Phase 2). A store pays to feature SKUs or pin its storefront in the marketplace. Flat fee, platform charge.
  4. Buylist spread (Phase 3). When a store buys a single from a collector through CollectX, the platform takes a small spread on the payout. This is the loop nothing else runs.
  5. CollectX for Business subscription (Phase 4). £19 / £49 / £99 tiers bundling the repricer, analytics, team seats and featured slots — each tier also lowers or waives the seller commission. The “pay us monthly, or pay per sale” lever.

Worked example — a collector buys a £100 card from a store

Item price£100.00
Buyer pays (item + 5% protection)£105.00
Store receives (item − 8% commission)£92.00
Stripe processing (~1.5% + 20p on £105)−£1.78
CollectX net per sale£11.22

For comparison: on eBay the same seller nets roughly £87 after ~13% fees. A CollectX store nets £92 and reaches buyers who have told us exactly what they’re hunting. The buyer pays about the same either way. That gap — better seller economics and qualified demand — is the pitch.

Build sequence

Five phases, lightest first. Each ends on a metric that has to move before the next one starts — this is a validation ladder, not a feature list. Economics is called out per phase so no phase ships without a way to pay for itself.

01

Verified storefront + bulk listing

Prove the demand side: will approved stores actually onboard and list, and does anything sell? Deliberately built on the existing listing model — ship fast, learn, then invest.

Ships

  • Branded /store/:slug — logo, banner, policies, shipping tiers, location; extends the existing /sellers/:userId page rather than replacing it.
  • “Verified store” badge on listings + a Stores filter in the marketplace. Distinct from collector reputation tiers.
  • CSV bulk importer: one upload creates the collection cards and the sale listings in a single pass. Runs on today’s ownership-transfer model — hacky, but it means a store can list 200 cards in a minute.
  • Store onboarding reuses the current Stripe Connect Express flow untouched.
Economics

Seller commission goes live via the fee-override table: 3% founder rate for the first cohort (hand-set per account), 8% default afterward. Buyer protection fee unchanged. First real store GMV lands here.

Effort ~2–3 sessions Risk med — ownership model strains at volume Metric 3 stores live · first store sale
02

Inventory engine + repricer

Replace the hack with the real thing. A store’s stock is a SKU ledger with quantities and price rules, not a pile of individual collection rows — and it re-prices itself nightly.

Ships

  • store_inventory (SKU = card + condition + grade, qty, cost basis, bin) and store_listings — a parallel path to marketplace_listings, the way orders parallels trades. Sale decrements quantity; no physical-row transfer.
  • store_price_rules + a nightly repricer (same cron shape as refresh-card-prices): “price at X% of TCGplayer market, floor £Y, never below cost”.
  • Store dashboard: sell-through rate, aged inventory, “12 SKUs priced below market” alerts, revenue by set.
  • Oversell guard: reserve-on-checkout + generous auto-cancel, since in-store sales won’t sync in real time yet.
Economics

The repricer and dashboard are free here to drive adoption — they become the headline benefit of the Phase 4 subscription. Promoted listings switch on: a flat fee to feature a SKU or pin the storefront. Commission unchanged.

Effort ~4–6 sessions Risk med-high — oversell & feed dependency Metric a store lists 500+ SKUs · repricer runs nightly
03

Buylist — the differentiator

Close the loop. Stores post what they’ll pay for singles; the collector’s own portfolio and want-list surface those offers. This is the thing eBay and TCGplayer don’t do, and it’s why a store picks CollectX.

Ships

  • store_buylist — a store sets buy prices as a % of market by set / rarity / condition, or per-card overrides, with a daily spend cap.
  • On a collector’s collection and want-list: “3 stores want this card — best offer £48. Sell now.” One tap creates a purchase order; collector ships; payout on receipt, same escrow rails as orders.
  • Reuses the want-list auto-match engine already in the app, pointed the other direction.
Economics

Buylist spread: the platform takes ~2% of the payout on a store’s buy. Low rate on purpose — the volume and the collector’s reason to keep the app open are the point. Also feeds store inventory, which feeds Phase 2 GMV.

Effort ~3–5 sessions Risk med — pricing abuse, condition disputes Metric first collector→store buylist sale · repeat sellers
04

CollectX for Business

Turn the tooling into recurring revenue and let real shops run their whole operation here.

Ships

  • Subscription tiers (£19 / £49 / £99) — repricer + analytics + team seats + featured slots, each tier lowering or waiving the seller commission.
  • Team accounts: store_members with owner / lister / shipper roles under one store_id.
  • Shopify / TCGplayer inventory sync (near-real-time oversell protection).
  • LGS discovery: a map of verified physical stores, local pickup option. UK-first — matches the GBP / Royal Mail rails already in place.
Economics

MRR becomes the primary line. The pitch to a store: “pay 8% per sale, or £49/mo and pay 3%.” High-volume stores self-select into the subscription; the platform trades variable take for predictable revenue and stickier accounts.

Effort ongoing Risk med — support load, KYC for businesses Metric first paying subscriber · MRR growth

Guardrails & risks

Don’t dilute the trading identity
CollectX’s pull is card-for-card trading and the collector tools. Store listings live behind a clearly-labelled Stores surface and never outrank peer trade listings by default. The two marketplaces stay visually and structurally separate.
Verification is the value exchange
A store gets the badge, the storefront and qualified demand only after a human checks business documents / resale certificate. Fake storefronts are a fraud vector — manual review in Phase 0–1, semi-automated later. Store sales never inflate a user’s peer trade count.
Oversell is what kills LGS marketplaces
Until POS sync exists (Phase 4), lean on reserve-on-checkout plus a generous auto-cancel-and-refund flow. Set the expectation with stores up front rather than letting a buyer pay for a card that sold at the counter an hour ago.
The pricing feed becomes load-bearing
The repricer and buylist both key off TCGplayer market data pulled via pokemontcg.io / tcgcsv.com — currently free and occasionally flaky. Once a store’s revenue depends on it, that dependency needs a fallback and, eventually, CollectX’s own sold-price feed built from store transactions.
Chargebacks land on the platform balance
Separate charges & transfers means a disputed store sale is a debit against CollectX, not the store. Hold a reserve on new stores; only release the commission-net payout after the escrow window; require Connect KYC for the business entity.

Two open calls before Phase 1

Which store first? UK LGS — local pickup, map discovery, matches GBP / Royal Mail rails. Slower, higher trust. Online store — one partner, deep catalogue, thousands of listings overnight. Faster liquidity. One online store for supply and 2–3 friendly local shops for the map story.

Founder 3% commission is nominal; the collector-side density is what makes the whole app worth using.

Schema appendix

Additive throughout. New store tables sit parallel to the collector ones — the same discipline that kept orders from destabilising trades. Nothing here changes an existing table’s shape except two enum values and one nullable column.

-- Phase 0 --------------------------------------------------------
ALTER TYPE public.app_role ADD VALUE 'store';

CREATE TABLE public.store_profiles (
  user_id          uuid PRIMARY KEY REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  slug             text UNIQUE NOT NULL,
  name             text NOT NULL,
  bio              text,
  logo_url         text,
  banner_url       text,
  website          text,
  location         jsonb,          -- {city,country,lat,lng} for LGS map
  policies         jsonb,          -- shipping tiers, returns
  status           text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'pending'
                     CHECK (status IN ('pending','active','suspended')),
  verified_at      timestamptz,
  commission_bps   int  NOT NULL DEFAULT 800,   -- 8%; founder rows set 300
  subscription_tier text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'none'
                     CHECK (subscription_tier IN ('none','starter','growth','pro')),
  created_at       timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
);

CREATE TABLE public.store_applications (
  id            uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id       uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  business_name text NOT NULL,
  registration_no text,
  country       text NOT NULL,
  website       text,
  volume_estimate text,          -- '<50/mo' | '50-500' | '500+'
  message       text,
  status        text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'submitted'
                  CHECK (status IN ('submitted','approved','rejected','needs_info')),
  reviewed_by   uuid,
  reviewed_at   timestamptz,
  created_at    timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
);

-- the fee hook the whole model runs on. checkout reads this,
-- then falls back to marketplace_fee_config.
CREATE TABLE public.account_fee_overrides (
  user_id                 uuid PRIMARY KEY REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  seller_fee_bps          int,
  buyer_protection_fee_bps int,
  note                    text,
  updated_at              timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
);

-- Phase 2 --------------------------------------------------------
CREATE TABLE public.store_inventory (
  id          uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  store_id    uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES public.store_profiles(user_id),
  card_id     text NOT NULL,
  condition   text NOT NULL,
  is_graded   boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false,
  grade_company text, grade_score numeric,
  quantity    int  NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  cost_basis  numeric(12,2),
  bin         text,
  price_rule_id uuid,
  updated_at  timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  UNIQUE (store_id, card_id, condition, is_graded, grade_company, grade_score)
);

CREATE TABLE public.store_price_rules (
  id           uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  store_id     uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES public.store_profiles(user_id),
  name         text NOT NULL,
  pct_of_market int NOT NULL DEFAULT 95,   -- 95 = list at 95% of market
  floor_gbp    numeric(12,2) DEFAULT 0.50,
  never_below_cost boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT true
);

-- store_listings mirrors marketplace_listings but is inventory-backed:
-- a sale decrements store_inventory.quantity, no user_cards transfer.

-- Phase 3 --------------------------------------------------------
CREATE TABLE public.store_buylist (
  id           uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  store_id     uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES public.store_profiles(user_id),
  scope        jsonb NOT NULL,     -- {set_id?, rarity?, condition?, card_id?}
  pct_of_market int NOT NULL,      -- e.g. 60 = pay 60% of market
  min_gbp      numeric(12,2),
  daily_cap_gbp numeric(12,2),
  active       boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT true
);
-- buylist_orders reuses the orders escrow rails, direction reversed.

-- Phase 4 --------------------------------------------------------
CREATE TABLE public.store_members (
  store_id  uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES public.store_profiles(user_id),
  user_id   uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES auth.users(id),
  role      text NOT NULL CHECK (role IN ('owner','lister','shipper')),
  PRIMARY KEY (store_id, user_id)
);

Implementation begins this session with the Phase 0 slice:

  1. Migration: 'store' role, store_profiles, store_applications, account_fee_overrides.
  2. Wire create-checkout-session to read account_fee_overrides before marketplace_fee_config, then redeploy.
  3. /for-stores pitch page and /store/apply form + service.
  4. /admin/store-applications review queue — approve grants the store role and creates the store_profiles row.

Everything after Phase 0 is gated on the application count moving. If stores don’t apply, the plan is wrong and cheap to have learned that.
